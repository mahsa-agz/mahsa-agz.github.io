About

About me

I'm a data scientist trained in computer science (B.Sc., Amirkabir University of Technology) and data science (M.Sc., KAIST, Human Factors and Ergonomics Lab).

My work moves between research and engineering: benchmarking computer-vision pipelines against ground-truth motion capture, building the data infrastructure that turns raw sensor recordings into something a clinician or researcher can act on, and shipping the result as a tool the intended user can actually operate.

I'm currently a data scientist and deputy team lead at 3R Innovation, working on DrSimon Engine and PDSS, the digital-phenotyping pipeline for child and adolescent mental health. Alongside the technical work, I've started taking on product-side tasks from our project manager: scoping, writing up decisions, and thinking about who the work is actually for. The questions adjacent to building (what to build, for whom, why now) interest me, and I'd like to grow in that direction without leaving the technical work behind.

Before research, I shipped backend systems for Iran's national digital cheque infrastructure. I care about clean abstractions, evidence over opinion, and tools that respect the people using them.

Curiosity helps me begin difficult problems; persistence helps me stay with them. Outside work I'm a fourth-degree Taekwondo black belt, former team captain, assistant coach, and national medalist.

I'm always happy to connect with people working in data science, behavioral AI, digital health, human-centered technology, or technical program leadership.

DrSimon Engine & Pediatric Digital Sensor Study (PDSS)

Building a multimodal system that turns raw sensor recordings into features and models clinicians and researchers can use, and that describes user behaviour as a sequence of events and detects anomalies.

  • Designed and built the per-(child × task) feature pipeline for 103 children: four sensor modalities (eye-tracking, keyboard, stylus, voice), ~30 tasks per child, 14,500+ raw files, ~1,500 features per row.
  • Chose session-level windowing over fixed 15s / 60s windows after a quality-check pass showed the latter would drop roughly half the tasks; this preserved 100% task coverage across modalities.
  • Diagnosed and patched three blocking bugs in a partner lab's feature extractor without modifying their code, using in-memory wrappers, which kept their version control intact and made every patch explicit.
  • Built a personal typing-rhythm baseline for each child to flag unusual sessions, such as a different user or a change in the child's state: a per-user generative model over 1.7M keystrokes from 787 children, scored as a log-likelihood ratio against a population model.
  • Predicted clinician-rated ADHD hyperactivity (ROC AUC 0.76, F2 0.65) and inattention (ROC AUC 0.72, F2 0.66) from five attention tasks, comparing 9 models with nested cross-validation and selecting by F2 rather than accuracy; hyperactivity severity regression reached Pearson r = 0.54.
  • Analysed, in 103 children across three tasks, which of 51 stylus measures reflect the child rather than the task, using mixed-effects models, ICC and variance decomposition; manuscript in preparation.
  • Mentor team members and review analyses; working alongside our project manager on product-side tasks (scoping, sizing and writing up decisions).

HFEL, KAIST

2024 – 2025

CV-based monocular 3D pose estimation for ergonomic risk assessment

When is an off-the-shelf pose model accurate enough to score ergonomic risk, and when does kinematic error change the decision?

  • Compared MediaPipe and MMPose against an OptiTrack motion-capture reference across three camera views (front, 45°, side) and nine static and dynamic industrial tasks with different levels of occlusion (20 participants).
  • Separated kinematic fidelity (joint positions and angles) from ergonomic-decision robustness, the degree to which kinematic errors actually change REBA / RULA risk classification.
  • MediaPipe was more accurate than MMPose overall, with intraclass correlations of 0.87 (REBA) and 0.92 (RULA) against the reference; occlusion produced the largest joint errors.
  • Authors: Aghazadeh & Xiong. Published in International Journal of Industrial Ergonomics, 116 (2026), 104061, DOI.

HFEL, KAIST

2024 – 2025

ErgoYar, a mobile ergonomic-assessment app for non-experts

Translating a benchmarked CV pipeline into a mobile tool an SME worker or supervisor can run without training.

  • Built a Flutter front-end and FastAPI / Supabase backend that turn a smartphone video into per-frame REBA / RULA scoring with an annotated pose overlay.
  • Designed the interface around progressive disclosure (overview → body part → joint detail) and body-part-specific feedback rather than a single composite score, so the density of per-joint output stays interpretable on a small screen.
  • Pilot usability study with 10 first-time users: mean SUS 80.5 (good usability, above the benchmark of 68), uMARS app quality 4.06/5, SUS–uMARS Pearson r = 0.71.
  • All ten participants completed the seven-task protocol without facilitator intervention.
